# Page Anatomy — Section Template + Conversion Evidence

One page, ONE conversion goal (start trial / buy / book demo / join waitlist).
Multiple competing CTAs distract; exceptions (dual CTA for different intent
levels, e.g. "Start free trial" + "View demo") must point at the same journey
(CXL, [call-to-action research](https://cxl.com/blog/call-to-action/)).

Choice research adds nuance: the famous jam study (6 options → 30% bought vs
24 options → 3%) is real, but the 2010 meta-analysis (Scheibehenne et al.,
JCR) finds the average choice-overload effect is small and appears mainly
under complexity. The rule to keep: limit DECISIONS (tiers, CTAs), because a
page is already a high-stakes decision — not because "fewer choices always
wins".

## The persuasion arc (top to bottom)

| # | Section                                         | Job                                                                      | Skip when                                              |
| - | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1 | Hero: headline + subhead + CTA + product visual | Who it's for, what outcome, how — 5-second test                          | never                                                  |
| 2 | Social proof strip                              | Instant credibility under the promise                                    | you have zero proof (use founder/BiP identity instead) |
| 3 | Problem agitation                               | Pain in the customer's exact words + cost of inaction                    | audience is most-aware (they know the pain)            |
| 4 | Mechanism / how it works                        | WHY the outcome is believable — your named mechanism + demo GIF/video    | never                                                  |
| 5 | Feature → benefit blocks                        | Concrete capabilities, each tied to an outcome                           | never (devs buy features, but translate them)          |
| 6 | Proof: testimonials, numbers                    | Specific outcomes from matching ICPs                                     | never — borrow trust if young                          |
| 7 | Pricing / offer                                 | What you get, enumerated; simple tiers; anchor; guarantee                | page goal is waitlist/trial signup                     |
| 8 | FAQ / objections                                | Answer the real hesitations (security, migration, "can it do X", cancel) | never                                                  |
| 9 | Final CTA + P.S.                                | Restate offer + risk reversal; last-chance summary                       | never                                                  |

This is the long-form direct-response skeleton (promise → problem → agitation
→ empathy → solution → offer → features-to-benefits → distributed proof →
objection handling → recap → CTA) as taught by Copyhackers. Copy-sequence
doctrines worth one rule each (all practitioner doctrine, not lab data):

- **Schwartz** (_Breakthrough Advertising_): the headline must match the
  traffic's awareness stage (table below) — mismatched headlines die unread.
- **Sugarman** ("slippery slide"): every sentence exists only to get the next
  one read; the first line's only job is line two.
- **Halbert** ("reason why"): specificity and a stated reason beat cleverness.
- **PAS / AIDA**: default skeletons for low-awareness traffic (Problem →
  Agitate → Solution; Attention → Interest → Desire → Action).

## Length: match to traffic temperature

Short copy correlates with higher conversion (Unbounce, below) — for WARM
traffic that already knows you. Cold traffic, high prices, and complex offers
need the objections answered in sequence on the page: long-form wins there
(Schwartz-derived doctrine; no public controlled dataset). Rule: the colder
and pricier, the longer; never pad — every section must do arc work.

## Hero section skeleton

1. **Headline** — the one big promise, matched to awareness stage (see table
   below). Wiebe: "Your headline is 80 cents out of your copywriting dollar" —
   spend ~50% of copywriting time here, draft 25-50 variants, then pick.
2. **Subhead** — the mechanism ("how"): resolves "ok, but how?".
3. **Visual** — the product itself (GIF/screen capture > abstract illustration).
   Video above the fold gets up to 50% more engagement (Wistia).
4. **CTA** — value-stating button + friction reducer next to it
   ("No credit card required · 2-minute setup").
5. **Micro-proof** — one line of numbers or logos directly under the CTA.

### Headline formulas that carry weight (Copyhackers)

- "The only way to \<desirable outcome\> without \<undesirable thing\>"
- "\<Hard thing\> in \<time\> or \<promise\>"
- "\<Desirable outcome\> like \<expert\> without \<expected downside\>"
- "\<Outcome\> even if \<objection\>"
- Mechanism-led for solution-aware traffic: "\<Product\> uses \<mechanism\> to \<outcome\>"

Clarity over cleverness always: "If you confuse, you lose." Specificity over
hype: "Join 46,700 others" / "Save $3,500 in 16 weeks".

### Headline by awareness stage (Schwartz)

| Audience stage | Headline leads with                      |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Unaware        | Identity call-out / story, never product |
| Problem aware  | The pain, their words                    |
| Solution aware | The mechanism (new way to the promise)   |
| Product aware  | Proof (numbers, users, comparison)       |
| Most aware     | The offer itself                         |

## Above the fold — evidence

- Visual credibility is judged in **50 milliseconds** and stable at 500ms
  (Lindgaard et al. 2006, [BIT](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01449290500330448))
  — the first viewport must look professional instantly.
- **57% of viewing time is above the fold, 74% in the first two screenfuls**
  ([NN/g scrolling & attention](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/));
  the top 20% of the page gets 42% of attention.
- People do scroll (66% of attention is below the fold — Chartbeat), BUT
  **35% of desktop users leave before scrolling at all**
  ([Chartbeat](https://chartbeat.com/resources/research/scroll-behavior-across-the-web/))
  — so the fold carries a "mini landing page" (promise + proof + CTA), and
  cut-off elements invite the scroll.
- A page must answer, in 5 seconds: what is it, who is it for, what do I do
  next (Copyhackers 5-second test — test for CLARITY only).
- Users scan in F / layer-cake patterns
  ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/))
  — headline + subheads must carry the argument scannably; body copy serves
  the readers the skim-path caught. Ogilvy doctrine: 8 of 10 read the
  headline, 2 of 10 read the copy.
- Video: 30s for an offer, 60s explainer, ≤90s testimonial (Wistia). 96% have
  watched an explainer, 85% were convinced to buy by video (Wyzowl 2026).

## Body copy — evidence (Unbounce Conversion Benchmark 2024: 41k pages, 57M+ conversions)

- Median landing-page conversion across industries: **6.6%**; SaaS median
  **3.8%**. The median is the bar to beat, not the ceiling.
- **5th-7th grade reading level converts at 12.9% in SaaS vs 2.1% for
  professional-level copy** — write plainly.
- Difficult words (3+ syllables) correlate with −24.3% conversion; reading
  time −19.4%; word count −18.6%. SaaS sweet spot: 250-725 words, ≤140
  difficult words.

## CTA buttons — evidence

- Name the get, not the action (Michael Aagaard, ContentVerve button tests,
  archived primary): "Order Information and Prices" → "Get Information and
  Prices" = +14.79% clicks, +38.26% on the sister-site replication.
  "Get Membership" → "Find Your Gym & Get Membership" (names the scenario)
  = **+213.16%** clickthrough — the "+68%" figure circulating for this test
  is not in the primary source.
- "Start your free 30 day trial" → "Start **my** free 30 day trial" = +90%
  CTR; direction varies by page (reversed −24.9% on a forum payment page) —
  flag "my vs your" for a cheap A/B, default to first person for
  trials/accounts.
- Outcome-stating, ≤5 words: "Send me the free guide", "Start my free trial"
  — the button completes the thought in the visitor's head.
- Ask of every CTA: what does the prospect want, and what do they GET when
  clicking? Name the get.

## Proof — evidence

- **49% of consumers trust reviews as much as personal recommendations**
  (BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026). What makes a testimonial
  convincing: backed by similar reviews (56%), describes a positive
  EXPERIENCE (46%), recent — within a month (44%), high star rating (42%),
  owner responded (37%).
- The worthless testimonial is vague, anonymous, outcome-free: "great
  product, love it!". The valuable one names a person matching the ICP, their
  before-state, and a measurable outcome.
- Zero customers yet → founder identity + credentials + a guarantee attached
  to your name (Halbert/Hormozi doctrine). Never fabricated counts. The full
  zero-proof ladder: first-customers `references/proof-and-trust.md`.
- "Reviews lift conversion up to 270%" (Spiegel) circulates only in listicles
  — do not cite it.

## Pricing / offer block — evidence

- **Decoy effect is field-validated**: Wu & Cosguner (Marketing Science 2020,
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2020.1231)) quantified it at a real
  online retailer. **Center-stage effect**: consumers infer the middle option
  is most popular and choose it more (Valenzuela & Raghubir, JCP 2009,
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2009.02.011)). So: ~3 tiers, mark one
  "recommended", place it center. Tier COUNT itself is convention.
- Showing the price at all: convention + qualification logic, not measured
  evidence — but hidden pricing is a documented top buyer frustration (see
  the cut list, item 6).
- **Risk reversal raises purchase intent**: return-policy leniency increases
  purchase intentions (meta-analytic review, Journal of Retailing 2016,
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretai.2015.11.002)); some leniency
  (especially long time windows) raises return rates too — monitor refunds.
  Put a dated, unconditional money-back guarantee adjacent to price and CTA.
- Value stack (Hormozi doctrine, aligned with the trust data): enumerate what
  they get — core + named bonuses with values — next to the anchor and the
  guarantee. Urgency only with a true reason-why (see cut list Tier C).

## Forms & friction — evidence

- Average US checkout carries **23.48 form elements vs an ideal 12-14**
  (Baymard); average cart abandonment **70.22%**, of which ~35% is recoverable
  through checkout UX alone ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)).
- Field count is about PERCEIVED friction, not raw count: removing 3 of 9
  fields DROPPED conversion 14% (cut the fields people wanted); rewriting the
  9 labels RAISED it 19.2% (CXL). Expedia deleted one field ("Company") →
  ~$12M/yr.
- Forced account creation is a top-4 abandonment reason (Baymard) — guest
  checkout or email-as-account.
- Single-column linear forms complete 15.4s faster than multi-column.
- Page speed: Vodafone +31% LCP → **+8% sales**; Rakuten's CWV work →
  **+53% revenue/visitor** ([web.dev case studies](https://web.dev/articles/why-speed-matters/));
  0.1s improvement → +8.4% retail conversions (Deloitte, secondary-repeat);
  53% abandon mobile pages >3s (Google/SOASTA, secondary-repeat). Target <2s.
- Mobile-first: **83% of landing-page visits are mobile**; mobile drives ~5×
  the visitors but desktop converts ~8% better (Unbounce CBR) — the mobile
  experience gap is where the money leaks.

## Visual cues

- Humans in imagery must look AT the CTA — a person looking away measurably
  reduces form fixation (deictic gaze, CXL eye-tracking). Hand-drawn arrows
  outperformed other cue styles. Auto-carousels/sliders underperform (NN/g;
  see cut list item 5) — one static hero visual beats a rotating one.

## FAQ / objection handling

Write FAQs from research, not imagination — each entry answers a phrase found
in reviews/support ("What almost stopped you from signing up?"). No public
RCT exists on FAQ placement or lift (practitioner consensus only). Standard
objection set for micro-SaaS: security/data ownership, export/migration
(lock-in fear), "can it do X" scope, cancellation/refund, what happens if you
(shutdown/acquisition) — solo-founder products get this one disproportionately;
answer it with data export + escrow-style promises you can keep.

## Craft rules (apply at edit time)

- One idea per sentence; one argument per section.
- Read aloud; kill throat-clearing openings.
- Every claim gets adjacent proof (numbers, logos, names).
- Never fabricate: invented testimonials/stats destroy the one asset a solo
  founder has — being a real, checkable person.
