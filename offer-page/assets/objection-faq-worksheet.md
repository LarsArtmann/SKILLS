# Objection → FAQ worksheet

One worksheet, two halves, two owners. **Part 1 (mining) is the strategy
half** — run it during VoC research (first-customers Step 2/3); if you have
no research corpus yet, stop and go do the research first, invented
objections fail the FAQ sourcing gate. **Part 2 (assembly) is the page
half** — run it when building or auditing the FAQ section of an offer page.

## Part 1 — Objection mining (first-customers half)

Sources, ranked by signal quality:

1. Support inbox / tickets — exact pre-purchase questions
2. Sales calls, DMs, and lost-deal notes — what the non-buyer said
3. Refund reasons — the objection that landed too late
4. Reviews: yours AND competitors' 1-3 star — objections at scale
5. Community/forum threads where the product category comes up
6. Waitlist / trial non-converters — ask them directly (one question: "what
   almost stopped you?")
7. Onboarding drop-off points — objections in behavior, not words

Extraction rules:

- **Verbatim phrasing only.** "Do you support SSO or is it password-only?"
  not "security concerns". Customer words are the voice-gate input;
  marketing paraphrase destroys them.
- One objection per row; record asker segment + rough frequency (n).
- Cluster near-duplicates into one row; keep the sharpest verbatim as the
  label.

Ranking: frequency × proximity-to-buy. An objection raised at checkout by
warm traffic outranks a loud objection from people who were never buyers.

Litmus test per objection — route it:

| It is really...                            | Route to                              |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| A question about the offer/fit/risk        | FAQ entry (Part 2)                    |
| A missing proof element ("anyone like me?") | Proof section, not FAQ               |
| A price/positioning mismatch ("too cheap to trust") | Offer/positioning, not FAQ   |
| A product gap ("does it do X?")            | Roadmap/TODO — never promise in FAQ   |

Assembly rules:

- Headline = the objection in the customer's words, turned into a question.
- Answer = 1-3 sentences, direct, first sentence answers it outright;
  end pointing back at the CTA ("Start your trial — cancel from day one").
- Placement: FAQ block after the offer/pricing block, before the final CTA;
  top-3 objections may also surface as one-line reassurances directly under
  the CTA they block.
- Anchor-link from the section each objection belongs to (pricing objection
  → link from the price block).
- Voice gates apply per entry: verbatim phrase, specificity, no "we're
  proud to", readable aloud in one breath.
- Sourcing gate: an entry with no mined source row behind it is INVENTED —
  cut it or go mine evidence for it.
