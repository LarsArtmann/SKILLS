# Proof, Trust & Risk Reversal

A solo founder selling a young product is asking for trust with thin evidence.
This file is the ladder: how to manufacture legitimate proof at zero customers,
how to present it, and how to reverse risk so trying costs nothing.

## The proof ladder (use the highest rung you legitimately have)

| Rung | Proof | Legitimate at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Paid customer testimonials with numbers | first paying users |
| 2 | Beta/pilot user quotes ("tested by 12 agencies on real audits") | pre-launch |
| 3 | Your own build-in-public receipts (usage numbers, revenue screenshots, changelog cadence) | day one |
| 4 | Live demo / interactive product on the page ("the product is the proof") | day one |
| 5 | Founder identity: real name, face, personal domain, about page | day one |
| 6 | Open-source code, transparent status page, public roadmap | day one |

At 0→1 rungs 3-5 ARE the social proof — a named human with a face and a public
track record converts where an anonymous "trusted by 10,000 teams" (fabricated)
would end the relationship. Never fabricate counts or testimonials; the
downside is asymmetric (permanent credibility loss, dark-pattern liability).

## Testimonial craft

- Specific outcomes beat praise: "cut our report prep from 6 hours to 40
  minutes" outperforms "love this product".
- Match the ICP: a testimonial from the visitor's exact role/segment sits near
  the section that addresses that segment's pain.
- Attribute fully: name, role, company, photo (and link if they permit).
  Anonymous testimonials read as invented.
- Ask with a question that extracts specifics, not adjectives: "What changed
  for you in numbers? What were you doing before?" (Copyhackers survey
  questions).
- Place proof at every point of hesitation: directly under CTAs, next to
  pricing, inside FAQ answers — not ghettoized in one "testimonials" strip.
- Video testimonials: ≤90s (Wistia guidance); even a Loom-style clip from a
  beta user reads as authentic.

## Micro case study structure (early products)

Problem (their words) → what they tried before → switch trigger → what
changed (number) → quote. Two of these on the page outperform six generic
blurbs.

## Guarantees & risk reversal

Purpose: move the risk of a wrong decision from buyer to seller. Standard
ladder:

1. **Unconditional money-back** (14/30/60 days) — maximum conversion lift.
   Widely reported practitioner experience puts software refund rates in the
   low single digits (heuristic — no rigorous public dataset; track your own
   from day one), and long-standing direct-response doctrine holds the
   guarantee pays for itself in added conversions (37signals/Basecamp
   famously ran unconditional guarantees as a core positioning weapon).
2. **Conditional / results guarantee** — "hit outcome X within 30 days or
   full refund". Stronger copy than #1 but requires the outcome to be
   observable and partially in your control; state the conditions plainly.
3. **Anti-guarantee** (no refunds, deliberately) — premium/scarcity
   positioning; only with proof to back it.

Micro-SaaS default: #1 with a plainly worded, one-paragraph refund policy
linked from the pricing section. Longer windows (60d) convert better than
30d with negligible refund-rate difference in most reports — if unsure, 30
days is safe. Honor refunds instantly; each honored refund is a refund-rate
statistic, not a war story.

Also reverse non-money risk: free tier/trial ("no card required"), data export
on every plan, one-click cancel. "Easy to leave" paradoxically makes staying
cheap to choose.

## Trust signals that carry weight (and the ones that don't)

Carry weight:

- Real founder identity: name + face + about page with history. For solo
  products this is the #1 trust asset — 89% say video/product quality affects
  brand trust (Wyzowl 2026) and design quality dominates first impressions
  (94% of first impressions are design-related, cited via CXL).
- Policy pages that exist and are readable: privacy, terms, refund. Absence
  reads as amateur at checkout.
- Security page for anything touching customer data: where data lives, who can
  access it, backup story. B2B buyers check this before email.
- Status page / public changelog — signals an operated, alive service.
- Data ownership + export statement near pricing (kills lock-in objection).

Weak (skip unless compliance requires): generic security badge clip-art,
"256-bit SSL" callouts (table stakes), stock photos, fake award logos. Trust
badges from unknown vendors show little reliable conversion lift; a payment
provider's known mark (Stripe/PayPal) at checkout is the one that matters.

## Ask-for-proof playbook (first 10 customers)

1. Over-deliver on onboarding for users 1-10 personally.
2. At the first success signal, ask the specific-outcome question.
3. Request permission to quote with attribution; offer founding-member
  treatment (locked price, name in changelog) as the exchange.
4. Ship the quote to the page the same day; tell them they're on it. The
  gesture itself generates goodwill → referral.
