# Founder Cold Outreach — the B2B Channel When You Have No Audience

Cold outreach earns its place only when: B2B ICP, no audience, and no
community where the buyers gather. If a niche community exists,
participating there beats any email volume ([./channels-and-launch.md](./channels-and-launch.md)).

## Why tiny-and-personal wins at 0→1

You need 10 customers, not 10,000 leads. A founder sending 20 researched
emails reads as a founder; a blast of 2,000 reads as spam and burns the
domain you'll want for launch emails. Reply rates to real cold email run
in the single digits (heuristic — treat 1 reply per 10 sends as a good
day), so the arithmetic: ~100 researched sends → ~10 conversations → 2-4
customers or pilot commitments. That IS the channel working.

## List building (an afternoon)

- Where your ICP is publicly visible: agency directories, conference
  speaker lists, GitHub orgs, LinkedIn, local business registries.
- Cap the first list at **50**. Every extra name lowers the average
  research quality, and research is the entire mechanism.
- Qualify by the trigger, not the size: who is feeling the problem THIS
  week (job post mentioning the pain, a public complaint, a launch) —
  triggered prospects reply multiples better than cold-but-perfect ICPs
  (heuristic).

## The email (3-5 sentences, one ask)

Structure:

1. **Subject**: about THEM, lowercase-casual ("quick question about
   [agency]'s client reporting").
2. **First line**: proof you looked — reference their specific situation,
   a page, a post. No compliments ("love your work" = template smell).
3. **The bridge**: the problem in THEIR vocabulary (from the phrase bank —
   [./market-research.md](./market-research.md)).
4. **The ask**: NOT "buy". Ask for the 10-minute demo, the feedback, the
   pilot: "Can I show you how it works for 10 minutes? If it's useless,
   tell me why — that's still useful to me."
5. **Signature**: founder name, product, one link in the PS, not the body.

Sent from the founder's personal address, plain text, no tracking pixels
(they leak through tools and read as spam), no attachments.

## The 3-touch sequence (standard heuristic)

| Touch | When    | Content                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | day 0   | the email above                                                                                                                                            |
| 2     | day 4-5 | one-line reply in the same thread: "any thoughts?" + one NEW angle or proof bit                                                                            |
| 3     | day 10  | breakup: "closing the loop — one question before I do: what would have made this relevant?" (replies from breakup emails are disproportionately the yeses) |

Then stop. A non-reply is an answer.

## Inside a reply: Mom Test, not a pitch

The first reply is a conversation, not a close. Ask about their current
process ([./market-research.md](./market-research.md) interview questions),
diagnose before offering, and offer to do the work manually once:
"I can set your first one up myself today." Concierge onboarding converts
the first 5-10 customers and produces the exact vocabulary for the page.

## Compliance floor

Cold B2B email is legal in most jurisdictions but regulated everywhere
(CAN-SPAM, GDPR business-interest carve-outs vary): real sender identity,
working opt-out, no misleading subject. Volumes of 50 researched emails
make this easy — blast tooling is what makes it both illegal-adjacent and
ineffective.

## Anti-patterns

- `{first_name}` template merges (the tell is always visible)
- Fake familiarity or manufactured coincidences
- Bought lists (stale, illegal-adjacent, and your domain reputation dies
  with the first blast)
- A second ask inside the first email (demo AND call AND forward to a
  colleague)
- Outsourcing "to an SDR" at 0→1 — the founder's voice IS the product's
  credibility
