# Sales Page Anatomy — Section Template + Conversion Evidence

One page, ONE conversion goal (start trial / buy / book demo / join waitlist).
Multiple competing CTAs distract; exceptions (dual CTA for different intent
levels, e.g. "Start free trial" + "View demo") must point at the same journey
(CXL, [call-to-action research](https://cxl.com/blog/call-to-action/)).

## The persuasion arc (top to bottom)

| # | Section                                         | Job                                                                      | Skip when                                              |
| - | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1 | Hero: headline + subhead + CTA + product visual | Who it's for, what outcome, how — 5-second test                          | never                                                  |
| 2 | Social proof strip                              | Instant credibility under the promise                                    | you have zero proof (use founder/BiP identity instead) |
| 3 | Problem agitation                               | Pain in the customer's exact words + cost of inaction                    | audience is most-aware (they know the pain)            |
| 4 | Mechanism / how it works                        | WHY the outcome is believable — your named mechanism + demo GIF/video    | never                                                  |
| 5 | Feature → benefit blocks                        | Concrete capabilities, each tied to an outcome                           | never (devs buy features, but translate them)          |
| 6 | Proof: testimonials, numbers                    | Specific outcomes from matching ICPs                                     | never — borrow trust if young (see proof-and-trust.md) |
| 7 | Pricing / offer                                 | What you get, enumerated; simple tiers; anchor; guarantee                | page goal is waitlist/trial signup                     |
| 8 | FAQ / objections                                | Answer the real hesitations (security, migration, "can it do X", cancel) | never                                                  |
| 9 | Final CTA + P.S.                                | Restate offer + risk reversal; last-chitchance summary                   | never                                                  |

This is the long-form direct-response skeleton (promise → problem → agitation
→ empathy → solution → offer → features-to-benefits → distributed proof →
objection handling → recap → CTA) as taught by Copyhackers.

## Hero section skeleton

1. **Headline** — the one big promise, matched to awareness stage (see table
   below). Wiebe: "Your headline is 80 cents out of your copywriting dollar" —
   spend ~50% of copywriting time here, draft 25-50 variants, then pick.
2. **Subhead** — the mechanism ("how"): resolves "ok, but how?".
3. **Visual** — the product itself (GIF/screen capture > abstract illustration).
   Video above the fold gets up to 50% more engagement (Wistia).
4. **CTA** — value-stating button + friction reducer next to it
   ("No credit card required · 2-minute setup").
5. **Micro-proof** — one line of numbers or logos directly under the CTA.

### Headline formulas that carry weight (Copyhackers)

- "The only way to \<desirable outcome\> without \<undesirable thing\>"
- "\<Hard thing\> in \<time\> or \<promise\>"
- "\<Desirable outcome\> like \<expert\> without \<expected downside\>"
- "\<Outcome\> even if \<objection\>"
- Mechanism-led for solution-aware traffic: "\<Product\> uses \<mechanism\> to \<outcome\>"

Clarity over cleverness always: "If you confuse, you lose." Specificity over
hype: "Join 46,700 others" / "Save $3,500 in 16 weeks".

### Headline by awareness stage (Schwartz)

| Audience stage | Headline leads with                      |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Unaware        | Identity call-out / story, never product |
| Problem aware  | The pain, their words                    |
| Solution aware | The mechanism (new way to the promise)   |
| Product aware  | Proof (numbers, users, comparison)       |
| Most aware     | The offer itself                         |

## Above the fold — evidence

- 91-100% of users scroll (Huge) BUT 84% difference in how they treat
  above-vs-below content (NN/g), and 66% of attention is still spent below the
  fold (Chartbeat) — so: make the fold carry a "mini landing page" (promise +
  proof + CTA), not everything.
- A page must answer, in 5 seconds: what is it, who is it for, what do I do
  next (Copyhackers 5-second test — test for CLARITY only).
- Video: 30s for an offer, 60s explainer, ≤90s testimonial (Wistia). 96% have
  watched an explainer, 85% were convinced to buy by video, 80% bought after a
  demo (Wyzowl 2026). Unbounce's price-increase video campaign: $660k ARR,
  annual-plan conversion 2% → 22%.

## Body copy — evidence (Unbounce Conversion Benchmark 2024: 41k pages, 464M visitors)

- Median SaaS landing-page conversion: **3.8%** (all-industry median 6.6%).
  Median is the bar to beat, not the ceiling.
- **5th-7th grade reading level converts at 12.9% in SaaS vs 2.1% for
  professional-level copy** — write plainly.
- Difficult words (3+ syllables) correlate with −24.3% conversion; reading time
  −19.4%; word count −18.6%. SaaS sweet spot: 250-725 words, ≤140 difficult
  words.
- Email converts best as a source (19.3%); LinkedIn converts 4-5× worse than
  Meta platforms.

## CTA buttons — evidence

- "Value + Relevance = More Conversions" (Michael Aagaard): "Order" → "Get"
  +38.26%; "Get membership" → "Find gym and get membership" +68%.
- First-person, outcome-stating, ≤5 words: "Send me the free guide", "Start my
  free trial" — the button completes the thought in the visitor's head.
- Ask of every CTA: what does the prospect want, and what do they GET when
  clicking? Name the get.

## Forms & friction — evidence (CXL)

- Field count is about PERCEIVED friction, not raw count: removing 3 of 9
  fields DROPPED conversion 14% (cut the fields people wanted); rewriting the
  9 labels RAISED it 19.2%. Expedia deleted one field ("Company") → ~$12M/yr.
- Single-column linear forms complete 15.4s faster than multi-column.
- Email verification steps kill signup (spam folder) — skip or make
  non-blocking (ProductLed onboarding checklist).
- Page speed: 0.1s improvement → +8.4% retail conversions (Deloitte
  "Milliseconds Make Millions"); ~-7% conversion per 1s delay; 53% abandon
  mobile pages >3s. Target <2s load.
- Chat: live chat lifts conversion ~20%; chatters are 2.8× more likely to buy;
  50% abandon when they can't get a quick answer (Forrester). For a solo
  founder: a chat widget + fast replies is a genuine conversion tool at 0→1.

## Visual cues

Humans in imagery must look AT the CTA — a person looking away measurably
reduces form fixation (deictic gaze, CXL eye-tracking). Hand-drawn arrows
outperformed other cue styles. Auto-carousels/sliders underperform (46% have
UX issues, Baymard); one static hero visual beats a rotating one.

## FAQ / objection handling

Write FAQs from research, not imagination — each entry answers a phrase found
in reviews/support ("What almost stopped you from signing up?"). Standard
objection set for micro-SaaS: security/data ownership, export/migration
(lock-in fear), "can it do X" scope, cancellation/refund, what happens if you
(shutdown/acquisition) — solo-founder products get this one disproportionately;
answer it with data export + escrow-style promises you can keep.

## Craft rules (apply at edit time)

- One idea per sentence; one argument per section.
- Read aloud; kill throat-clearing openings.
- Every claim gets adjacent proof (numbers, logos, names).
- Never fabricate: invented testimonials/stats destroy the one asset a solo
  founder has — being a real, checkable person.
