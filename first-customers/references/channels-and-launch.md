# Channels & Launch — Where the First Customers Come From

The aggregate answer from 497 Indie Hackers founder interviews: the first 10
paying customers come overwhelmingly from a **nameable, reachable group — warm
network plus one niche community you already participate in — plus a launch
spike**. Paid ads and SEO pay later, not at 0→1
([channel analysis](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/11-marketing-channels-that-consistently-work-for-founders-rBltLPaWjLQ9xHZiSqOW)).

## Channel decision (audience you already have?)

| Situation | First channel | Second |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You have an audience/followers/list | Launch post to them (email ≈ 19.3% LP conversion — Unbounce 2024) | Product Hunt after the soft launch |
| No audience, niche community exists for the problem | Participate (weeks), then share as a member | Directories |
| No audience, no community, B2B | Founder cold outreach (personalized, tiny volume) | Communities of your ICP |
| Developer tool | Show HN + relevant subreddits | Open source a piece |

One channel at a time (Rob Walling's stair-stepper rule) until it produces a
repeatable number of customers, then add the second.

## Soft launch → public launch sequence

1. **Pre-launch page** with email capture (the Buffer move: pricing buttons on
   a page with no product — Pieter Levels' favorite demand test; "the only
   real validation is people paying").
2. **Warm soft launch**: personal network + tiny community post. First 5-20
   users, fix what breaks, collect the first testimonials.
3. **Public launch**: Product Hunt / Show HN / Reddit, one per week, never the
   same day, each with its own tailored post.
4. **Re-launch**: every meaningful feature/anniversary is a launch (Levels:
   keep re-launching). Marc Lou's ShipFast did ~$6k in its first 48h on PH
   after a year of build-in-public priming — the launch converts the audience
   the channel didn't build.

## Product Hunt

- Numbers to expect: ~10k visits from a good launch, <10% of them even try the
  product (levels.io experience) — PH is a spike + badge + backlink, not a
  business.
- Mechanics: launch early in the day PT so the full day accumulates
  engagement; the maker posts a first comment telling the build story; answer
  every comment for 24h; badge on the site afterward.
- Prepare: assets (GIF demo, gallery), a shipped product (not a waitlist —
  launch-day visitors must be able to actually try it), and your warm audience
  notified WITHOUT asking for upvotes (asking friends to upvote is against
  HN/PH culture and backfires algorithmically).

## Hacker News — Show HN (official rules, news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html)

Fetched verbatim requirements:

- "Show HN is for something you've made that other people can play with."
- Must be **personally worked on, non-trivial** ("don't post quickly-generated
  one-offs"), and you must **be around to discuss** it.
- "Make it easy for users to try your thing out, **ideally without barriers
  such as signups or emails**."
- **Off topic**: blog posts, sign-up pages, newsletters, landing pages,
  fundraisers — "Don't post landing pages."
- "Please don't ask friends to upvote or comment."
- Present work with "how and why" (the linked HN talk "tips"): lead with what
  it does and what's interesting technically; plain speech, no marketing
  language; engage skeptics with data.

## Reddit / communities

- Self-promo is policed: the 9:1 rule (be a genuine contributor the other 9
  times) and subreddit-specific rules. Accounts created on launch day read as
  press releases and get filtered.
- Choose the niche sub where the PROBLEM lives (r/Bookkeeping, not r/SaaS)
  when possible; r/SideProject and r/InternetIsBeautiful accept maker posts
  broadly.
- Format that works: the honest build story with numbers, answering every
  comment, no link in the title (put it in a comment or profile).

## X / build in public

Marc Lou: "Twitter is big. That's where I get my first users all the time" —
the two tactics that worked at even ~1k followers: (1) a catchy headline that
challenges the status quo, (2) a killer demo video. Post the process (build
clips, numbers, mistakes); the audience converts at launch. Levels' version:
launch publicly, share revenue numbers, iterate loudly — transparency IS the
marketing for solo products.

## Directories (low effort, slow trickle)

Submit once, forget: Uneed, BetaList (pre-launch), SaaSHub, AlternativeTo
("alternative to X" traffic), Futurepedia / AI directories if AI-adjacent,
plus every niche directory for your category. Expected value: single-digit
visitors/day each, compounding backlinks; do them in one batch, never
re-submit.

## Founder cold outreach (B2B with no audience)

- Tiny volume, high personalization: research each prospect, reference their
  specific situation, 3-5 sentences, one clear ask (often "can I show you this
  for 10 minutes?" not "buy").
- Send from a personal address as the founder; spam tooling kills both
  deliverability and the founder-voice advantage.
- Realistic: reply rates in single digits; 20 thoughtful emails/day beats 2000
  blasted — at 0→1 you need 10 customers, not 10,000 leads.
- Follow the Mom Test inside the conversation: ask about their current process
  before pitching.

## What NOT to spend on at 0→1

- **Paid ads**: CACs have risen ~55% in five years pre-2022 and keep climbing
  (ProfitWell via ProductLed); ads need a converting funnel + LTV headroom you
  don't have yet.
- **SEO**: real but slow (6-12 months); start the content engine, expect
  nothing at 0→1. Exception: "alternative to \<competitor\>" pages when a big
  competitor exists — build them early, they rank easier.
- **Brand/social-media presence everywhere**: one platform, done properly,
  beats five abandoned accounts.
